🥗 All Vitamins

Learn about vitamins, their uses and food sources.

Vitamin A

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports normal vision, skin and immune function.

Food sources: Carrots, sweet potatoes, eggs and leafy greens.

Caution: Avoid high-dose supplements unless advised by a healthcare professional.

Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Helps the body convert food into energy and supports nerve function.

Food sources: Whole grains, beans, seeds and pork.

Caution: Ask a healthcare professional before using high-dose supplements.

Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports energy production and normal cell function.

Food sources: Milk, eggs, mushrooms and almonds.

Caution: Supplements are not a replacement for a balanced diet.

Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Helps the body release energy from food and supports skin and nerve health.

Food sources: Peanuts, fish, chicken and whole grains.

Caution: High-dose niacin can cause side effects; seek medical advice.

Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Helps the body produce energy and make certain hormones.

Food sources: Chicken, mushrooms, eggs, lentils and avocado.

Caution: Food sources are generally sufficient for most people.

Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports protein metabolism, red blood cell production and nerve function.

Food sources: Chickpeas, bananas, potatoes, fish and poultry.

Caution: Long-term high doses may damage nerves.

Vitamin B7 (Biotin)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Helps the body process fats, carbohydrates and proteins.

Food sources: Cooked eggs, nuts, seeds and legumes.

Caution: Biotin supplements can interfere with some blood test results.

Vitamin B9 (Folate)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports DNA formation and red blood cell production.

Food sources: Leafy greens, beans, lentils and citrus fruits.

Caution: People who may become pregnant should ask a healthcare professional about folic acid.

Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports nerve health, DNA production and red blood cell formation.

Food sources: Milk, eggs, fish, meat and fortified foods.

Caution: Vegans may need fortified foods or supplements after professional advice.

Vitamin C

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports collagen formation, wound healing and iron absorption.

Food sources: Oranges, guava, amla, tomatoes and peppers.

Caution: Very high supplement doses may cause stomach upset or kidney stones in some people.

Vitamin D

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Helps the body absorb calcium and supports bones and muscles.

Food sources: Oily fish, egg yolks and fortified foods.

Caution: Excessive supplements can cause dangerously high calcium levels.

Vitamin E

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Acts as an antioxidant and helps protect cells.

Food sources: Almonds, sunflower seeds, vegetable oils and spinach.

Caution: High-dose supplements may increase bleeding risk, especially with certain medicines.

Vitamin K

Click photo to enlarge

Uses: Supports normal blood clotting and bone health.

Food sources: Spinach, broccoli, cabbage and other leafy greens.

Caution: People taking warfarin should keep vitamin K intake consistent and consult their clinician.
